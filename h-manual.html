HiKOKI Standalone Battery Diagnostic & Service Tool

A portable OLED diagnostic tool for compatible HiKOKI / Hitachi BSL lithium-ion battery packs, including supported Multi Volt models. Read battery information, inspect recorded BMS history and use supported lock-recovery functions.

Important: A successful read or unlock does not mean that a battery is electrically safe. Inspect the battery and its cell groups before returning it to use. Never reset a battery to bypass a genuine physical fault.

Getting started

Quick Start

What the device does

  • Communicates with compatible HiKOKI battery electronics without requiring a computer.
  • Displays available pack and cell-bank measurements.
  • Displays BMS state, protection records and reported usage history when supported.
  • Provides a supported BMS unlock/recovery operation for eligible locked packs.

What it cannot repair

  • Failed, deeply discharged or internally shorted cells.
  • Broken welds, damaged connectors, fuses or sense wires.
  • Defective temperature sensors or damaged BMS circuitry.
  • Physical, water, heat or impact damage.
  1. Inspect the battery for visible damage, heat, corrosion and abnormal condition. Do not connect a suspect or unsafe pack.
  2. Attach the HiKOKI battery using the correct mating contacts or adapter. Keep the connection steady.
  3. Move the device’s rocker power switch to ON.
  4. Use the single metal pushbutton to begin the battery read using the on-screen menu.
  5. When the read completes, review the Battery Summary, then navigate through the other diagnostic pages.
  6. If the battery is locked, investigate the underlying reason before considering the supported unlock function.
No computer required: Normal reading and the supported recovery function are performed directly on the handheld device.
Hardware

Buttons, OLED and Power Switch

OLED screen

Displays menu prompts, measurement values, status information and diagnostic pages. The display is not touch-sensitive.

Metal pushbutton

Use the button to operate the on-screen menu and move between available pages or actions. Follow the displayed prompts before initiating a service operation.

Rocker switch

Controls the device’s internal battery supply. Switch OFF when storing or transporting the handheld unit.

The illuminated button ring is a device indicator; it is not an independent indication that the attached battery is healthy.

How to interpret this list

These model names are recognised by the available firmware identification table. Recognition alone does not confirm that every battery revision supports all diagnostic readings, or that the unlock function is available for that battery. The specific BMS generation and protocol must be supported.

Not battery entries: BSL36200 (Multi Volt tool adapter) and ET36A (AC/DC adapter) have separate codes in the identification table; they are not included in the 20 battery models above.

Important: A recognized model number is not a safety approval or a promise that a locked pack can be recovered. If a battery cannot be read reliably, the tool must show a read/error condition rather than guessed values.
Diagnostics

Reading a Battery

  1. Connect the battery correctly and power on the handheld device.
  2. Start the read from the displayed menu using the pushbutton.
  3. Keep the battery connected while data is being received and decoded.
  4. The tool presents the main summary after the read. Navigate to further pages for detailed information.
  5. Check that reported battery voltage, bank values and status are physically plausible. An impossible value is not a valid measurement.
Data validity: Battery and cell data must come from a valid BMS response. If a field is unavailable, the read fails, or values contradict the measured pack, treat that field as unknown and independently verify it. Do not rely on suspicious counters or voltages when making a repair decision.
On-screen information

The Five Diagnostic Pages

Page 1

Battery Summary

The primary view after a read. Provides the available overall pack measurements, identification and detected operating/BMS state.

Use this to decide whether the battery requires more detailed investigation; it is not a pass/fail load test.

Page 2

Cells & Banks

Shows supported cell/bank information, including available individual bank values and the lowest/highest reading where provided by the BMS.

Multi Volt cell/bank presentation depends on the specific pack architecture. Do not interpret a bank value as an individually measured cell unless confirmed.

Page 3

Protection History

Displays stored protection events and counters where the battery exposes them, for example recorded over-discharge or over-temperature conditions.

Past event records are not proof that a fault is currently active.

Page 4

BMS Status

Shows BMS-related state and protection/lock information returned by the pack. Use it alongside voltage, temperature and history, not as the only safety check.

Page 5

Tool History

Shows stored tool-use information when the pack supports it, such as tool family identifiers and usage counts.

Counters are historical records and may be unavailable, overflowed or undecodable on some BMS generations.

Interpreting missing data

Not every BMS exposes every field. A blank, unavailable or failed reading is not zero, and it should not be converted into a guessed value.

Service function

Battery Lock Recovery / Unlock

Where supported by the battery and firmware, the handheld tool can perform a BMS unlock operation. The function addresses compatible electronic lock conditions; it does not repair damaged cells or circuitry.

Before an unlock

  1. Read the pack first and inspect its displayed status, voltage, cells/banks and protection history.
  2. Check the pack’s physical safety and rule out damaged cells, abnormal voltage, dangerous imbalance, overheating and wiring faults.
  3. Only if the model and condition are eligible, select the on-screen unlock/recovery option and follow the device prompts.
  4. Do not disconnect the pack or switch off the tool during an active recovery operation.
  5. Afterward, perform a fresh read. Check the reported state and verify behavior using the appropriate external equipment.
Never repeatedly unlock a re-locking battery without finding the cause. A pack can re-enter protection minutes or days after recovery because a persistent BMS condition, stored fatal history, or a physical battery fault remains. Repeated attempts do not establish safety or a permanent repair.

A completed unlock is confirmation of a service command outcome only. It is not a warranty of charger acceptance, output availability, capacity or long-term reliability.

Internal device battery

Power, Charging & Sleep

Internal rechargeable battery

The handheld unit contains its own rechargeable lithium battery, so normal use does not require a computer or external power supply.

Power switch

Use the rocker switch to power the unit on for normal operation. Switch off after use to avoid unnecessary internal battery drain.

USB charging

Charge using the device’s fitted charging connection and a suitable regulated USB 5V supply. Observe the actual charging indicators on your unit and do not leave a damaged device charging unattended.

Display sleep

The OLED can switch off after approximately three minutes of inactivity to reduce display power use. A blank OLED is not necessarily the same as the rocker switch being off.

Charging behavior: Connecting external power may wake the display even when the rocker switch is OFF. A lit display by itself does not prove that the internal battery is charging. Confirm charging using the indicator provided on the finished unit.
Support

Troubleshooting

The tool does not turn on

Check the rocker position and internal battery charge. Use a known-good suitable charging cable and power source. If the display remains blank, do not open the unit while connected to a pack.

The battery is not detected or the read fails

Check correct battery contact alignment, cleanliness and stable connection. Try a fresh read. A damaged BMS, unsupported battery revision or communication fault may prevent access. Never treat a failed read as a healthy battery.

Voltage or history values look impossible

Stop relying on the affected field. Verify voltage using a suitable meter and check whether the model/BMS generation is supported by the decoder. Do not invent substitute values or assume a reported maximum counter is real.

The battery unlocks, then locks again

This can indicate a recurring protection condition, unresolved hardware fault or stored BMS state. Investigate the genuine underlying cause; do not use repeated unlock attempts as a substitute for diagnosis.

The charger rejects the battery after an unlock

Check compatibility, terminals and pack condition, and verify the live voltage and BMS state. Charger acceptance is separate from an unlock-command result.

OLED goes blank after inactivity

The display may have entered its approximately three-minute sleep mode. Use the normal control to wake or restart the unit, as appropriate.

Some history or cell fields are missing

Different battery generations expose different data. A field not returned or not reliably decoded must be treated as unavailable, not zero.

Safety & limitations

Important Battery Safety Information

  • Never connect swollen, leaking, smoking, excessively hot, punctured or visibly damaged batteries.
  • Do not bridge or short pack terminals. Keep metal tools and loose wires away from live contacts.
  • Use appropriate independent electrical and thermal checks before deciding a battery is safe to charge or use.
  • Do not bypass protection, attempt recovery or return a pack to service when its cells or safety systems are defective.
  • Displayed data represents what the electronics successfully returned and the firmware reliably decoded. It is not a capacity test, load test or independent battery safety certification.
  • Store and transport lithium batteries in accordance with relevant safety practices and local regulations.
